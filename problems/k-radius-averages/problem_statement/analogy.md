Think of a street of houses, each with a number on it — say, how many trick-or-treaters it got. For every house, you want a "neighborhood score": the average of its own number plus the k houses on either side. Houses within k of either end of the street can't get a score — they don't have a full neighborhood on both sides — so they just get a −1.

The prefix-sum trick is the odometer move. Instead of re-adding 2k+1 houses for every single house, you walk the street once and write down a running total at each stop. After that, the sum of any stretch is one subtraction: the running total at the right end minus the running total just before the left end. Same as a road trip — you don't re-drive the segments to know how far you went between mile 40 and mile 90, you just subtract the two odometer readings.
